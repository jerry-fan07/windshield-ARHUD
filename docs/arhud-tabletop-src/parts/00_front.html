Windshield AR HUD · HUD-only TFT LCD edition

Tabletop Design

Two 16-inch monitors and a sheet of teleprompter glass: one monitor shows a road, the HUD software detects the road in that picture, and the other monitor's reflection lays the registered overlay onto it. The design of the rig, the detection, the display chain, their calibration, and the tests that prove each part before anything goes into a car.

Edition of 2026-10-27. Extends build stage B6 (Stage 0) of docs/ARHUD-BUILD-GUIDE.pdf and follows the plan in docs/ARHUD-PLAN-TFT.pdf and its specification docs/ARHUD-PLAN-TFT-SPEC.md. Shared numbers are imported from the plan's calc.py; bench numbers are derived in docs/arhud-tabletop-src/tcalc.py. Source of this document: docs/arhud-tabletop-src.

Before anything else

What this document is

The request was to build the tabletop display with the two YODOIT monitors and to design, in detail, the detection of the road and the display on the monitor that is reflected. This document reads that request one way, states it, and designs to it: monitor unit 1 lies face up under the glass as the picture generator (the PGU), exactly as build stage B6 places it; monitor unit 2 stands upright behind the glass, where B6 had a black backdrop, and shows the road. The HUD software runs its perception on the same road frames that unit 2 is showing, so the overlay the viewer sees in the glass is drawn from a road the software actually detected, registered to that picture. The result is the plan's whole software chain, from camera frame to lit pixel, running on a desk and measurable to a fraction of a degree, weeks before a car, a stereo camera or a custom mirror exists.

It is a design, not a build log: nothing here has been measured on the rig yet, and the document says at each number whether it is derived, assumed or still to be read off the hardware. It does not replace B6; it extends it, and where it departs from B6 it says why.

How to read it

Part I is the rig: its geometry, the light through the glass, what the viewer sees and how bright. Part II is the detection: where the road pictures come from, how lanes, vehicles and pedestrians are found in them and lifted to the car frame, and how that is scored. Part III is the display: how the renderer puts a car-frame point onto the PGU so that its reflection lands on the right pixel of the road picture, the timing of the chain, the calibrations, and the camera-in-the-loop variant. Part IV is the build: the software layout, the bring-up stages T-A to T-I with their gates, the risks, the bill of materials, and the appendices.
