12.1 Things that will go wrong, and what to do

Symptom or riskCause and response
The overlay slides against the road when the viewer movesPlanes not coplanar (section 3.1). Shim the road monitor or the PGU until the parallax check passes; a rail stop that drifts is the usual culprit.
The reflection is dim, far under 30% of the PGU's whiteThe PGU's polarisation is p to the glass (section 3.5). Retarder film, or rotate the PGU 90° and the picture in software; meter again.
Every line is doubled, {{ghost_px}} px apartThe glass's bare face is toward the viewer. Turn the glass over; if both faces are bare, accept the ghost on the bench and use thick lines, as the plan's windshield rule does.
The road picture is blurry at the eyeExpected for comma2k19 (× {{c2k_up}}); use the comma three route or a phone clip for demos; perception is unaffected.
The picture is at half resolution or offset on a monitormacOS scaling: the window was created at logical, not physical, pixels, or the display is set to "larger text". Use the high-DPI flag and native resolution; verify by photographing a 1 px checkerboard.
Judder on the road monitorA 20 Hz source on a 60 Hz display shows each frame for three vsyncs; a dropped vsync shows as a 4-vsync frame. display logs every schedule; look for a slow decode (software HEVC path) and force the hardware decoder.
hudd drops framesVsync stacking or a compositor frame; the warp mesh rebuilt every frame; another window on the PGU's display. B6.8 applies.
Chain over 90 msFind the stage with the probe's split measurement (byte-to-patch for render plus PGU lag; vsync-to-patch for the road monitor's lag). A portable monitor with two frames of scaler lag at each end eats 67 ms by itself; if so, record it and note that the car's HDMI board is a different device.
Compensation makes it worseThe sign (B6.5), or hudd's target set to tphoton while measuring against the picture (section 6.2): switch the target to the picture's time for registration tests.
Lane residual grows with range on comma2k19Pitch or height wrong (Table 5.1). Refit the vanishing point on a straight stretch; try the height as a free parameter against the lane width (3.7 m) at 20 m.
The networks score well on MetaDrive and badly on real clips, or the reverseExpected; MetaDrive's paint is synthetic and its score is a floor. Weight the real-clip agreement with modelV2 and the hand-checked frames; the plan's fine-tuning decision belongs to G1.
The PGU gets hot face up in the well1,200 nits in a closed well with a hood. Leave the well's ends open, run the PGU at the gain the condition needs rather than at full white, watch for dimming.
A monitor drops out on the Mac's portPower: use the monitors' own PD chargers; check which ports of the MacBook support two displays.
The Mac's inference times are nowhere near the Jetson'sThey are not meant to be; the bench times size the bench's own chain. The plan's budget is measured under B2 on the Jetson.
The loop camera sees the HUDIt is inside the reflection's cone: raise it above the glass's top edge, or hood the gap between glass and camera.
Two hosts disagree on timechrony not synced, or a topic stamped with the wrong clock. The photodiode chain is clock-free; use it to find which host's stamps are off.

12.3 Decisions this design leaves open

  • Eye-to-image distance: {{d_img}} mm by design; Table 3.1 for the others. Settled at T-C by looking.
  • Glass orientation (coated face): settled by the ghost photograph at T-A or T-G.
  • Whether the PGU needs the retarder film: settled by the photometry at T-G.
  • Which lane network: settled by score at T-D, as the plan intends.
  • Whether a larger glass is worth buying so the whole PGU reflects: only if a wider window than the plan's is wanted on the bench.
  • When the Jetson joins: after B2 (reflash); T-I is written so that it can run on the Mac with a webcam first.
