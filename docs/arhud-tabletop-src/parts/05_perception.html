5Detection design

The bench runs the plan's perception processes on the source frames: a lane network, an object detector with a tracker, and the lifting of their pixels to the car frame. Only the lifting differs from the car, because there is no stereo: it uses the recording's camera model and a static ground plane, the plan's own fallback mode, which the bench makes measurable.

5.2 Frames and their path

On one host the frame travels by a shared-memory ring: roadsrc writes the source image once, publishes a small roadFrame message (index, capture time, slot, camera model id) and the networks read the slot. Across two hosts (the Jetson for perception, the Mac for the road monitor) the frame goes as JPEG over TCP at the source's rate, which costs a few milliseconds and is measured as part of the chain. The networks never see the enlarged picture on the road monitor; they see the source at its native size, as the car's networks will see the ZED's frame. For the camera loop (chapter 9) the camera's frame replaces the source image and a homography maps its detections into source pixels before lifting, so planed and everything after it are unchanged.

5.3 The lane network

The plan's order stands: Ultra Fast Lane Detection v2 first (MIT, CULane weights, 1,600 × 320 input crop, ONNX export in its repository), CLRNet or CLRerNet second (Apache, higher F1, slower), CondLaneNet and YOLOP as alternatives. The bench's contribution is the measured choice: each candidate runs on the same 300 frames with truth (MetaDrive) and on the same comma three segments against modelV2, and the score of §5.7 decides. Inputs are the source frame resized to the network's crop with the horizon placed where the network's training data had it; outputs are row-anchored image points with a confidence per line. On the Mac the networks run through ONNX Runtime's Core ML provider or PyTorch on Metal; on the Jetson through TensorRT FP16 from the same ONNX file, so the bench score and the car's networks are the same weights.

5.4 The detector and the tracker

YOLO11s at 640 px, FP16, the plan's eight COCO classes; exported once to ONNX (and to Core ML for the Mac). ByteTrack on the CPU for identities and the per-track velocity the renderer advances by. The alternatives the plan names (RF-DETR Nano, DashCamNet) are tried only if YOLO11s misses the recall criterion on the bench. Signs: the crop classifier for US speed-limit values is a stretch goal here as in the plan; the bench has the frames to train it on later.

5.5 Lifting to the car frame

For a pixel (u, v) in a camera of focal length f, principal point (cx, cy), height h and pitch θ (down positive): the ray's depression below the axis is α = atan((v − cy)/f), its range along the ground z = h / tan(θ + α), and its lateral position y = z · (u − cx)/f (to first order; the code intersects the ray with the plane exactly). Lane points are lifted row by row and the far end is cut where the row-to-range step exceeds a metre; a vehicle's range is its box base lifted the same way; a pedestrian's likewise. In the car, roadd replaces h and θ by the fitted plane every frame and the stereo range inside 35 m; the lifting function is the same.

Objects are not smoothed beyond the tracker's own state.

5.6 Ego-motion

comma2k19 gives CAN speed and the IMU gyro; a comma three route gives carState; MetaDrive gives the truth; a phone clip gives GNSS speed or nothing. egod publishes whichever exists at 100 Hz by interpolation, flags the source, and sets fix quality so that hudd's rules (prediction off without a fix, speed dash after 1 s) can be exercised by cutting the stream on a key press, the F7 scenario of the road simulator on real data. The plan's GNSS-latency parameter is measured in the car; on the bench the recording's own timestamps are exact.

5.7 Scoring

Against MetaDrive truth, the plan's gate G1 as written: ego lane found in ≥ 95% of frames; lateral error ≤ 0.15 m at 10 m, 0.30 at 20, 0.50 at 30 (seen from the camera these are {{lat_10_deg}}°, {{lat_20_deg}}° and {{lat_30_deg}}°, or {{lat_10_px}}, {{lat_20_px}} and {{lat_30_px}} px on the road monitor); vehicles in the ego lane within 50 m recalled ≥ 90%; pedestrians within 30 m ≥ 85%; range within 10% of truth. Against comma three routes, agreement with modelV2's lane lines at the same ranges, reported but not gated, since the reference is itself a model. The scorer works in the source's image, by lifting the truth through the same camera model and comparing in the car frame, so perception error and lifting error can be separated by also scoring in pixels.
