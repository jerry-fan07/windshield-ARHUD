2Geometry and parts

Every dimension of the rig follows from one rule: the glass's centre is the same distance from the PGU's face as from the road monitor's face. Then the reflection of the PGU lies on the road monitor, the two pictures are one plane, and the viewer may sit anywhere in front of the glass without the overlay sliding.

2.1 The monitors

Both are the owned YODOIT 16-inch panels: {{mon_px_w}} × {{mon_px_h}} px, which the plan takes as {{ppi}} ppi ({{pitch_um}} µm per pixel, {{mon_w}} × {{mon_h}} mm active, a {{mon_diag_in}}-inch diagonal). A true 16.0-inch diagonal would be 282.8 ppi and {{mon_w_283}} × {{mon_h_283}} mm. assumed Measure the active width with a rule in T-A; every millimetre in this chapter scales with it, and the pixel pitch enters the calibration file. The vendor's peak white is {{l_mon}} cd/m² and the contrast 1,200:1 or 2,000:1 depending on the listing verify; both are metered in T-G. One of the two is attached to the MacBook Pro today and reports {{mon_px_w}} × {{mon_px_h}} at 60 Hz; the Jetson drove the other over HDMI on 2026-10-07, so HDMI input exists on at least that unit.

2.2 The glass

The Neewer NA17 teleprompter beamsplitter: {{glass_w}} × {{glass_w}} × {{glass_t}} mm tempered glass, 70% transmitted and 30% reflected, with an anti-reflection coating on one face, about $53. It is the cheap one of the plan's pair and the one B6 names. At 45° its footprint along the viewing axis and its height are both {{glass_foot}} mm, which just covers the PGU's short side ({{mon_h}} mm); its width is {{glass_short}} mm less than the monitors' {{mon_w}} mm, so {{unreflected_side}} mm of the PGU at each side is never reflected and {{glass_px_w}} px of its {{mon_px_w}} are usable. The HUD window needs {{win_w}} mm; the margin is large. If the larger glass the plan lists for Stage 1 is bought instead (a 30R/70T sheet of about 16 × 21 inches), the whole PGU is reflected; nothing else changes.

Which face carries the beamsplitter coating and which the anti-reflection coating is not stated on the vendor page. decision in T-A, by test: photograph a thin bright line on the PGU through the glass in both orientations and keep the one with the fainter second image (section 3.6).

2.4 Enclosure, stands and the slide

B6's enclosure serves with two changes. The backdrop becomes the road monitor's stand. And the glass cannot ride in slots cut into B6's cheeks: those stand {{mon_w}} + 10 mm apart so the PGU fits between them, and the NA17 is only {{glass_w}} mm wide. So a base of 6 mm plywood or black foam board about 750 × 450 mm; two cheeks {{mon_w}} + 10 mm apart with a well between them where the PGU lies face up on felt, its cable out the back; and on each cheek's inside face a 23 mm spacer block carrying a grooved batten at 45°, the two grooves {{glass_w}} + 2 mm apart, so the glass seats with its centre {{d_glass}} mm above the PGU's face. The battens start {{clear_low}} mm above the PGU's face, so the spacer blocks clear the monitor. (The larger glass the plan lists for Stage 1 would reach B6's cheek slots directly.) Every inside face matte black or flocked. Behind the glass, a 300 mm drawer slide or a camera macro rail carries the road monitor on a block {{r_bottom}} mm high, with a millimetre scale along the slide and a stop at the coplanar position, so the parallax variant (section 3.8) is a slide to a number and back. A hood of black card over the top keeps room light off both monitors and off the glass.

At the eye point, a chin rest or a tripod with a quick-release plate at the glass centre's height, {{d_eye}} mm from it. The measuring camera (a phone with manual exposure, or a mirrorless camera) mounts there for every calibration and test; the viewer sits there for the demo.

2.5 Hosts and cables

The MacBook Pro M4 drives both monitors from the start: it is attached to one now and the model supports two external displays verify. Both run at native {{mon_px_w}} × {{mon_px_h}} and 60 Hz, as separate displays, with macOS scaling off for the windows that matter (chapter 10); auto-brightness, True Tone and Night Shift off for the externals. The monitors' own power: a USB-C PD charger each, because a laptop port may not sustain full brightness verify. Every process of the bench runs on the Mac on one clock. When the Jetson returns it takes the PGU over HDMI and runs perception; the Mac keeps the road monitor and roadsrc, and the two hosts share a clock over Ethernet (chapter 7).
