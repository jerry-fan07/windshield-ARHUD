6Distortion and colour

A single lens used as a strong magnifier has two flaws that grow toward the edges of the field. Both are large enough to see, and both are corrected in software rather than optics, which is how the plan already intends to handle distortion.

Pincushion distortion

A magnifier enlarges the edges of the field slightly more than the centre, so a straight grid on the panel looks bowed outward, pulled toward the corners (Figure [[N distortion]]A). Traced, the corner of the 80 × 40 mm crop appears {{dist_corner_mm_deg}}° further out than a perfect lens would put it. The cure is to draw the content bowed the other way, pulled inward into a barrel shape, by the amounts in Figure [[N distortion]]B: {{dist_corner_mm_abs}} mm at the crop’s corner, {{dist_corner_abs}}% of its distance from the centre. This is the plan's pre-distortion mesh, and the plan measures it directly by photographing a rendered grid from the eye point, so these numbers are a check rather than an input.

Lateral colour

PMMA bends blue light slightly more than red (its refractive index is {{n_b}} at 450 nm and {{n_r}} at 620 nm). At every place where the lens bends light, blue is bent a little further, and the lens bends hardest toward its edges. So the blue copy of the picture comes out a little larger than the red one, and a thin white line near the field edge spreads into a small rainbow, blue on the outside (Figure [[N latcolour]]). Traced, the split is {{lc_half}}° halfway out and {{lc_edge}}° at the field edge: {{lc_edge_arcmin}}′, or {{lc_edge_px}} pixels on the 5.5-inch panel.

Glass would not fix it: BK7 gives {{lc_par_bk7}}° against PMMA's {{lc_par_pmma}}°. Polycarbonate would double it ({{lc_par_pc}}°), which is one reason PMMA is the right plastic. The fix is in software, as in VR headsets: draw the red, green and blue images through three slightly different pre-distortion meshes, so that after the lens they land on top of each other. Two cautions. The split depends on where the light crosses the lens, which moves with the head: with the eye 50 mm off-centre, even the middle of the field splits by about 0.2°, so the colour meshes, like the geometric one, should follow the head tracker. And an LCD's primaries are bands about 40–60 nm wide, not single wavelengths, so each colour still smears by up to {{lc_resid_frac}} of the uncorrected split ({{lc_resid}}° at the field edge, for typical colour-filter bands, ASSUMED) after the correction.
