11Building, aligning and checking it

Packaging: fold the path

With the lens on the dash and the combiner above it, a straight 280 mm drop puts the panel inside the dashboard (Figure [[N car]]). A front-surface mirror at 45° under the lens folds the path: the panel stands upright at one end of a low box, its light runs along the box, turns up at the mirror and passes through the lens (Figure [[N fold]]). The optics are unchanged because the path length is unchanged; the image is mirrored once, which the renderer undoes. The mirror must be front-surface (coated on top) for the same reason the combiner has a ghost: a rear-surface mirror would add a second reflection. This packaging is a suggestion; the plan does not say how Stage 1 is mounted.

Setting the panel distance by parallax

Section 4 showed that the focal length is uncertain by ±2% and that the image distance follows it closely, so set the panel distance on the lens actually used. The easiest reference is a physical target at the intended image distance: a pole or a cross on a stand, {{d_eye_img}} m from the eye, in line with the HUD. Show a cross on the panel at the target's position, look past it at the target, and move the head from side to side (Figure [[N align]]). If the cross slides against the head movement, the image is nearer than the target: move the panel away from the lens. If it slides with the head, the image is farther: move the panel toward the lens. When the cross stays on the target, the distance is right to within a few centimetres.

A useful cross-check is the focal length itself. Hold the lens, grooves toward a distant lamp (more than 20 m away; never the sun), and move a white card behind it until the lamp's image is sharpest. The card is then about f − {{zh}} mm from the flat face. That number gives the starting panel distance from Figure [[N tolerance]]A before the parallax check.

Checklist

  1. Lens: precision grade, PMMA, grooves facing up toward the combiner (section 7). Ask the supplier whether the facets are curved and whether an anti-reflection coating is available (sections 5 and 8).
  2. Panel on a slide with about 15 mm of travel around 280 mm; mirror and panel fixed to well under 0.5 mm once set.
  3. Measure f with a distant lamp; set the panel by parallax against a target at {{d_eye_img}} m after the rig has warmed up; record the result for the renderer.
  4. Calibrate as the plan describes (eye point, virtual-plane projection, pre-distortion mesh), adding a per-colour mesh and storing the measured image distance across the field, not one plane (sections 4 and 6).
  5. Inside the box: matte black everywhere, light only the crop, keep the rest of the panel black (section 8).
  6. Combiner: measure its reflection and the back-surface ghost with the panel's own polarisation, in both orientations (section 9).
  7. Sun: lid closed whenever the HUD is off; never leave the rig in a parked car in the sun; sun-watch rule and thermistor as in Stage 2; thermocouple test of the empty box before daytime use (section 10).
  8. Cleaning: water and a soft brush or microfibre on the grooved face; no ammonia or alcohol cleaners on PMMA.
