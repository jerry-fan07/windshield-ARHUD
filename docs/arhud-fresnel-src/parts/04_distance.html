4Where the image sits, and how stable it is

Because the panel sits so close to the focal point, small changes move the image a long way. Figure [[N vofu]]A plots the image distance against the panel distance. At 280 mm the slope is {{dvdu}} mm per mm: moving the panel 1 mm toward the focal point pushes the image about 0.2 m farther away, and with the panel at 295 mm the image is already {{v_295}} m away. In metres this looks alarming.

The eye, though, does not focus in metres. It focuses in dioptres, one over the distance in metres, and on that scale the curve is gentle and nearly straight (Figure [[N vofu]]B): {{diopt_per_mm}} D per mm of panel movement. A relaxed eye sees everything within about ±0.25 D of its focus equally sharply, which here covers panel positions from about 260 mm to beyond 295 mm. So the exact image distance hardly matters for how sharp the HUD looks. What it does matter for is parallax, explained below.

The lens's tolerance, and its temperature

Two things move the image even when the panel stays put. The first is the lens itself: the focal length of a stock Fresnel lens is only specified to ±2% for the precision grade and ±5% for the standard grade (Knight Optical). A precision lens could therefore put the image anywhere from {{tol_v_p2}} m to {{tol_v_m2}} m; a standard one from {{tol_v_p5}} m to {{tol_v_m5}} m (Figure [[N tolerance]]A). The fix is simple: treat 280 mm as a starting point, mount the panel on a slide, and set its distance for the lens in hand (section 11). For the precision grade the slide needs about {{tol_u_m2}}–{{tol_u_p2}} mm of travel.

The second is temperature. Warming PMMA makes it expand ({{alpha}} parts per million per kelvin) and lowers its refractive index (about {{dndt}} × 10−4 per kelvin); both lengthen the focal length. A lens 40 K warmer than when it was set has f = {{th_f_40}} mm and puts the image at {{th_v_40}} m instead of 4.2 m (Figure [[N tolerance]]B). A glass lens would hardly move. In practice the cabin at night is fairly stable, but the lens sits right above a 12 W backlight in Stage 1's box, so set the distance after the rig has warmed up, and keep the box ventilated.

Why the distance matters: parallax

A HUD symbol is drawn on the virtual image, about 5 m away; the car it marks is perhaps 30 m away. Seen from one head position they line up. Move the head sideways and they separate, the way a finger held up at arm's length jumps against the background when you switch eyes (Figure [[N parallax]]). The separation is the head movement times (1/Dimage − 1/Dtarget): {{par_30m}}° for {{head}} mm of head movement, half the plan's 1.0° registration budget. The plan's head tracker cancels this by redrawing the symbol for the new eye position, but the correction is only as good as the image distance the software assumes. If the lens is 2% long and nobody measured it, {{head}} mm of head movement leaves {{par_tol_p2}}° of error; a 40 K warm-up leaves {{par_th_40}}°. Both fit inside the budget, but they are avoidable: measure the image distance once (section 11) and give it to the renderer.

The image is curved

So far the image has been treated as a flat picture {{d_eye_img}} m away. The ray trace says otherwise. Toward the edges of the field the light passes the lens at a slant, the effective panel distance grows, and since the panel is close to the focal point the image runs farther away: {{isurf_c}} m at the centre, {{isurf_w_t}} m (tangential) to {{isurf_w_s}} m (sagittal) at the side edges and up to {{isurf_k_s}} m at the corners (Figure [[N isurf]]). The two values at each point are astigmatism: lines running around the centre come into focus at one distance, lines pointing at the centre at another.

In dioptres all of this lies between {{isurf_k_D_s}} and {{isurf_c_D}} D, and the two foci at the corner are {{astig_D}} D apart, well inside the eye's ±0.25 D, so the image looks sharp everywhere. But the parallax argument above applies point by point: a symbol near the edge of the field sits on an image up to {{edge_extra_m}} m farther away than one in the middle. The renderer should use a traced or measured image distance for each part of the field rather than one plane, the same conclusion the project's Cycles checks reached for Stage 2's mirror.
