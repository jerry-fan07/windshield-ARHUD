3How the lens makes a virtual image

Hold a magnifying glass close to a page and the print looks larger and farther away. Stage 1 is the same trick at a larger scale: the panel sits just inside the lens's focal length, so the lens cannot bring its light to a focus. Instead, the light leaves the lens spreading out, as if it came from a large picture far behind the panel. The eye cannot tell the difference between light that really came from there and light that only appears to, so it sees the large picture. Optics calls it a virtual image: nothing is actually at that place, and a screen held there would show nothing.

Figure [[N magnifier]] constructs the image with three easy rays. A ray leaving the panel parallel to the axis is sent through the far focal point F′. A ray through the centre of the lens goes straight on. A ray that starts as if from the near focal point F leaves parallel to the axis. After the lens the three rays spread apart and never meet; traced backward, they meet at one point behind the panel, and every other ray from the same panel point appears to come from that same point.

With the plan's numbers, the lens equation 1/v = 1/f − 1/u gives v = −{{v}} mm: the image is {{v_m}} m from the lens, on the panel's side (the minus sign). It is {{M}} times larger than the panel, so the 80 × 40 mm crop becomes {{img_w}} × {{img_h}} m. These figures are measured from the lens. The driver's eye is another {{eye_z}} mm back, so the image is {{d_eye_img}} m from the eye, and its angular size, traced through the lens model with its distortion, is {{fov_eye_w}}° × {{fov_eye_h}}° rather than the plan's 16° × 8°. Seen from the eye, the 4K monitor's 872 pixels across the crop give {{ppd_mon_eye}} pixels per degree ({{arcmin_px_mon}}′ each, at the eye's limit of about 1′), and the 5.5-inch panel's {{px_55}} give {{ppd_55_eye}} ({{arcmin_px_55}}′ each).

Why the image is so far away and so large

Both |v| = uf / (f − u) and M = f / (f − u) have f − u underneath. The panel is only {{sun_focus_beyond}} mm short of the focal length, so f − u is small and both are large. Moving the panel toward the focal point sends the image off toward infinity; moving it toward the lens brings the image in and shrinks it. Section 4 is about how sensitive that is.

A magnifier does not change brightness. The image is larger, but the light from each panel point is spread over a proportionally larger image area, and the eye receives the same luminance it would get from the panel itself, minus the lens's losses (section 9). That is why the HUD's brightness has to come from the panel and its backlight, not from the optics.
