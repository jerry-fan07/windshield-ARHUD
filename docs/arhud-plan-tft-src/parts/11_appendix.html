A.1 Fresnel reflectance

For light from air (n₁ = 1) reaching glass (n₂ = 1.52) at incidence angle θᵢ, with the transmitted angle θt from Snell's law, the reflected fractions are:

sin θt = sin θᵢ ÷ n₂ Rs = [(cos θᵢ − n₂ cos θt) ÷ (cos θᵢ + n₂ cos θt)]² Rp = [(n₂ cos θᵢ − cos θt) ÷ (n₂ cos θᵢ + cos θt)]²

At θᵢ = 65°: sin θt = 0.906 ÷ 1.52 = 0.596, so θt = {{theta_t}}°. Then Rs = {{rs65}} and Rp = {{rp65}}, a ratio of {{rs65_x}}. Rp is zero at the Brewster angle, arctan(1.52) = {{brewster}}°. These are per surface. A laminated windshield has two: the first reflects Rs, and the second returns (1 − Rs)² Rs after two passes through the first, so the second image carries {{ghost_rel}} of the first's brightness and the two together reflect {{rs65_two}}.

A.2 Ghost separation

A ray entering the inner surface at θᵢ refracts to θt, crosses the laminate of thickness t, reflects off the outer surface, and returns to the inner surface a distance 2t·tan θt along the glass from where it entered. It exits parallel to the first reflection, so the two reflected rays are parallel and separated, perpendicular to their direction, by that distance times cos θᵢ:

t = 2.1 + 0.76 + 2.1 = {{t_glass}} (assumed build; the interlayer's index is taken as the glass's) along the glass: 2 × {{t_glass}} × tan {{theta_t}}° = {{ghost_surf}} between the rays: {{ghost_surf}} × cos 65° = {{ghost_perp}} seen at image distance D: atan({{ghost_perp}} ÷ D) D = 2 m: {{ghost_2}}′ D = 2.5 m: {{ghost_2_5}}′ D = 7.5 m: {{ghost_7_5}}′ D = 10 m: {{ghost_10}}′

Two parallel copies of a diverging bundle are seen as two copies of the image displaced by the separation at the image's own distance, which is why the angle falls with D. Check against a published example: Lumetrics' note on wedge-angle measurement works a case with t = 4.9 mm, 65° incidence, the image 2 m beyond the glass and the eye 0.75 m from it, and reports a separation of 0.0643°. The formula above with D = 2.75 m gives {{ghost_lum_check_deg}}°, or {{ghost_lum_check}}′. One arcminute at 7.5 m is {{arcmin_mm_75}} mm. The wedge angle that would make the two rays converge at the eye is set by the glass maker and is not derived here.

A.3 Mirror equation, Stage 2, and the PGU width table

For a concave mirror of focal length f, an object at distance u in front of the mirror forms an image at distance v, with 1/u + 1/v = 1/f. Magnification is M = −v/u. When the object is inside the focal length, v is negative and the image is virtual, on the object's side, and magnified.

Virtual image at 7.5 m, f = {{f2}}: 1/u = 1/f + 1/7500 = 0.0030303 + 0.0001333 so u = {{u2}} M = 7500 ÷ 316.1 = {{m2}} image width = 60 mm × 23.7 = {{img2_w}} so field 2·atan(0.711 ÷ 7.5) = {{fov2_w}}° image height = 30 mm × 23.7 = {{img2_h}} so field 2·atan(0.356 ÷ 7.5) = {{fov2_h}}° pixels per degree = ({{crop_px_w}} px across the crop) ÷ {{fov2_w}}° = {{ppd2}} For another active width w, keeping the same image width at 7.5 m: M = 1.42 m ÷ w; u = 7.5 m ÷ M; 1/f = 1/u − 1/7500 w = 40 mm: M = {{w40_M}}, u = {{w40_u}} mm, f = {{w40_f}} mm w = {{panel_w}} mm (the full panel width): M = {{w110_M}}, u = {{w110_u}} mm, f = {{w110_f}} mm

The mirror's aperture does not depend on the PGU: it must cover the eye box plus the field at the mirror's distance from the eye. At an assumed 1 m along the folded path: {{mir_w}} × {{mir_h}} mm, from 130 + 2 × 1000 × tan({{fov2_w}}°/2) and 70 + 2 × 1000 × tan({{fov2_h}}°/2).

A.5 Window size

The width of a window of field θ at distance D is 2D·tan(θ/2). At 7.5 m, the 10° window is 2 × 7.5 × tan 5° = {{win_10x4_w}} wide. The 4° height is {{win_10x4_h}}, and the 5° height is {{win_10x5_h}}. Dividing by the magnification {{m2}} gives the crop: a 10° × 4° window is a {{crop_10x4_w}} × {{crop_10x4_h}} mm crop of the panel.

A.6 The luminance chain and the panel requirement

An ideal imaging element changes an image's size but not its luminance, so the chain from the panel to the eye is a product of the reflectances along it:

windshield path: 0.90 × 0.90 × 0.236 = {{path_ws}} (two mirrors, one glass surface, s-polarised) both surfaces: 0.90 × 0.90 × 0.37 = {{path_ws_two}} combiner path: 0.30 (Stage 0 and 1) panel needed for 1,500 at the eye: 1,500 ÷ {{path_ws}} = {{need_panel_day}}; with both surfaces {{need_panel_day_two}} panel needed for {{l_road_eye}} at the eye: {{l_road_eye}} ÷ {{path_ws}} = {{need_panel_road}} panel needed for 50 at the eye: {{need_panel_night}} custom backlight at {{l_panel_design}}: {{bl_ws}} at the eye, {{bl_headroom}}× the sky target; beats an ambient of {{bl_ws}} ÷ 0.15 = {{bl_amb}}

The "ambient it beats" column of section 6.5 is each eye luminance divided by 0.15, the overlay share required by the 1.15 contrast ratio.

A.7 Contrast ratio and the two targets

The plan defines contrast as (overlay + background) ÷ background. For a background of 10,000 cd/m² and a required ratio of 1.15, the overlay must supply 0.15 × 10,000 = 1,500 cd/m² at the eye. For a sunlit road at an assumed 5,000 cd/m² the overlay must supply {{l_road_eye}}.

A.9 Camera-to-eye baseline and range error

An object at range z, seen by a camera offset from the eye by a baseline b perpendicular to the line of sight, lies at a bearing that differs between the two by about b ÷ z. If the range is read as z + Δz, the eye-side bearing is computed as b ÷ (z + Δz), and the error is:

Δθ ≈ b ÷ z − b ÷ (z + Δz) ≈ b · Δz ÷ z² b = {{b_ce}}: z = 10 m, Δz = 1 m: {{reg_10_1m}}°; z = 20 m, Δz = 2 m: {{reg_20_10pct}}°; z = 30 m, Δz = 3 m: {{reg_30_10pct}}°

A.10 Stereo range error, and the pitch fallback

Stereo range follows from disparity, z = f·B ÷ d, so an error δd in disparity gives δz = z²·δd ÷ (f·B). With the 4 mm lens at 1080p (80° horizontal, f = 960 ÷ tan 40° ≈ 1,144 px), B = 0.12 m, and an assumed δd = 0.25 px:

z = 10 m: {{sdz_10}} m ({{sdz_10_pct}}); 20 m: {{sdz_20}} m ({{sdz_20_pct}}); 30 m: {{sdz_30}} m ({{sdz_30_pct}}); 50 m: {{sdz_50}} m ({{sdz_50_pct}}) registration from the stereo error at 30 m: {{sreg_30}}°; at 10 m: {{sreg_10}}°

Fallback mode with a static ground plane: a camera at height h sights a road point at distance z at depression atan(h ÷ z). A pitch error ε moves the back-projected point to z′ = h ÷ tan(atan(h ÷ z) + ε). For h = 1.30 m, z = 20 m, ε = 1°: z′ = {{pitch_z1}} m, an error of {{pitch_dz}} m, which through A.9 is {{pitch_reg}}° at the eye.

A.11 Parallax, motion compensation, and latency sums

A head shift of Δ moves the line of sight to a point at distance d by atan(Δ ÷ d) if the image is not corrected. For Δ = 0.150 m: at 7.5 m, {{par_7_5}}°; at 10 m, {{par_10}}°; at 30 m, {{par_30}}°.

An eye at height h = 1.22 m sights a road point at horizontal distance d. The depression angle is θ = atan(h ÷ d). At 30 m/s the car travels {{travel}} in 100 ms, so the point's distance drops from 10 m to 7.0 m:

Uncompensated: θ(10 m) = {{dep0}}°; θ(7.0 m) = {{dep1}}°; change = {{uncomp}}° Residual for unmodelled a = 1 m/s² over t = 0.1 s: Δx = ½ a t² = {{resid_mm}}; Δθ ≈ h ÷ (d² + h²) × Δx = {{resid_deg}}° Residual for a speed error of 1 m/s over 0.1 s: Δx = 0.1 m; Δθ = {{resid_speed_deg}}°

The five timed latency stages sum to a minimum of 10 + 10 + 1 + 8 + 8 = {{lat_min}} ms and a maximum of 30 + 25 + 3 + 16 + 25 = {{lat_max}} ms. At 30 m/s these are {{lat_travel_min}} m and {{lat_travel_max}} m of travel. The head tracker is excluded because it affects parallax, not the road-frame content.

A.12 Sun focus, and the sun's entry direction

The sun subtends 0.53° (9.25 mrad). Its focused image at the focal plane is about f × 9.25 mrad = 330 × 0.00925 = {{sun_focal}} mm across. For the ray-trace estimate, parallel on-axis rays strike a spherical mirror of radius {{r2}} over a 300 × 200 mm aperture. Each reflected ray is intersected with the panel plane {{u2}} from the vertex. The radius that contains 90% of the rays is {{r90_pgu}} mm at that plane ({{r90_focus}} mm at the paraxial focal plane, because marginal rays focus nearer the mirror).

concentration C = (300 × 200 mm²) ÷ (π × r90²) ≈ {{conc_pgu}} collected power = 0.300 m × 0.200 m × 1,000 W/m² × {{ws_t_sun}} = {{sun_w}}; spot ≈ {{sun_spot_mm}} mm; {{sun_wcm2}} W/cm² after the cold-mirror fold alone (visible share {{vis_frac}}, assumed): {{sun_after_cold}} after the cold mirror and the panel-side IR-cut glass ({{filt_frac}}, TI DLPA096's filter budget): {{sun_after_filt}} after the aperture polariser (passes {{pol_t}} of unpolarised light, assumed): {{sun_after_pol}}; {{sun_after_pol_wcm2}} W/cm² ≈ {{sun_suns}} suns

Entry direction. Take the windshield as a plane raked α = {{rake}}° above horizontal with its top toward the rear, and the eye ray to the HUD patch depressed δ = {{lookdown}}° below horizontal. Reflecting the eye ray's direction in the glass gives the direction of the ray that continues to the mirror: δ − 2α = {{lookdown}} − 56 = −{{mirror_dir}}°, that is {{mirror_dir}}° below horizontal, forward and down, into the dash. The incidence angle on the glass is the angle between the eye ray and the glass normal, {{inc_check}}°, consistent with the 65° design point. The mirror's rays travel the reverse, backward and up at {{mirror_dir}}°, and the part that transmits through the glass continues in that direction, so the sun reaches the mirror only from {{sun_elev}}° above the horizon behind the car. The sky patch that can reach the panel is the eye box as seen from the glass plus the field: 2·atan(65 ÷ 750) + {{fov2_w}} = {{patch_w}}° wide and 2·atan(35 ÷ 750) + {{fov2_h}} = {{patch_h}}° tall, with the eye-to-glass distance of 750 mm assumed. The sun moves 15° per hour, so it crosses the patch's width in about {{patch_minutes}} minutes at a fixed heading.

BGlossary

TermMeaning in this plan
AccommodationThe eye's change of focus with distance.
Age (of a message)Current time minus the capture time of the sensor frame the message came from.
BacklightThe light source behind a liquid-crystal cell. Here, a custom direct-lit LED unit behind the crop.
BeamsplitterPartly reflecting, partly transmitting glass. A 70/30 part transmits 70% and, in the teleprompter convention, reflects 30%.
BEFBrightness enhancement film: a prism film that narrows a backlight's emission cone and raises its on-axis luminance.
Brewster angleThe incidence angle at which the p-polarised reflection of a surface is zero.
Cold mirrorA mirror coating that reflects visible light and transmits infrared. Used as the fold mirror.
CombinerA partly reflecting glass that merges the image from a display with the view through it.
ConformalGraphics that must sit on the thing they describe, such as the ribbon on the lane.
Contrast ratioIn this plan, (overlay + background) ÷ background. Both targets use 1.15.
CropThe 60 × 30 mm region of the panel that is lit and drawn; the rest stays black.
DC-DC converterHere, the ignition-sensed supply that powers the Jetson and lets it shut down cleanly.
Dead reckoningCarrying a position and speed estimate through a satellite outage with inertial sensors.
DisparityThe pixel offset of a feature between the two images of a stereo camera; range is inversely proportional to it.
Eye boxThe region the eye can move through while still seeing the whole image.
Field of viewThe angle the image covers as seen from the eye.
Fresnel equationsThe formulas for reflected and transmitted fractions at a boundary between two media.
Fresnel lensA thin lens whose power comes from concentric grooves. Used as a magnifier in Stage 1.
Ghost imageThe second, fainter copy of the HUD image reflected by the windshield's outer surface.
GNSSGlobal navigation satellite systems; the receiver gives position and, from Doppler, speed.
Ground planeThe road surface as a plane in the camera's frame, fitted each frame from stereo depth.
Half-wave retarderA film that rotates linear polarisation by twice the angle between its axis and the light's.
Hot mirror, IR-cut glassA filter that reflects or absorbs infrared and passes visible light. Held in reserve for the panel side.
IMUInertial measurement unit: gyroscope and accelerometer; here, the one inside the ZED 2i.
KeystoneA trapezoidal distortion of an image from an off-axis mirror. Corrected in software by hudd.
LambertianA surface whose luminance is the same in every direction.
Luminance (cd/m²)Brightness of a surface as seen by the eye. Also called a nit.
Motion-to-photon latencyDelay from the moment the scene changes to the moment the image shows it.
Nominal eye pointThe design eye position, from which the calibration is measured.
PGUPicture generation unit: the device that makes the image, here the TFT panel with its backlight.
Pre-distortionA warp applied to the image so that the optics' own distortion cancels it.
PVB, wedge PVBPolyvinyl butyral, the plastic interlayer in laminated glass. A wedge-shaped version removes the ghost at the design eye point. Optional here.
RegistrationHow accurately a graphic lines up with the thing it marks.
Relay mirrorThe concave mirror that re-images the panel's picture into the windshield. Stage 2.
RibbonThe translucent fill between the driver's two lane lines. The demo's hero element.
s- and p-polarisationThe two perpendicular polarisations of light. The windshield reflects s much more strongly.
Sun-watchThe rule that closes the shutter when the computed sun direction falls inside the mirror's entry patch.
TensorRTNVIDIA's inference runtime, which runs the lane and object networks on the Jetson's GPU.
TFT LCDThin-film-transistor liquid-crystal display: a shutter array in front of a backlight.
TopicA named stream of messages on the HUD's bus, such as lanes or ego.
TrackerThe step that gives each detected object a stable identity and a velocity across frames.
Virtual imageAn image that the eye perceives at a distance, though no light originates there.
ZeroMQ, msgpackThe messaging library and the serialisation format of the HUD's bus.
