8.3 Power and wiring

The Jetson runs from the car's 12 V accessory socket through the ignition-sensed converter, which lets it see the key-off and shut down cleanly after a configurable delay. The backlight's LED driver runs from the same converter output, with its dimming input driven by the controller board; a 5 V buck or the Jetson's USB powers the panel's board. The cameras and the GNSS board draw from the Jetson's USB ports. Mini PC 2 and monitor 2 run from a USB-C PD charger. Nothing is connected to the car except the 12 V feed, and that feed is fused.

Two things the previous plan had to verify are settled by this design. The audience display no longer needs a second output from the Jetson, because it is a video stream over Ethernet to mini PC 2. And there is no USB Ethernet adapter on a comma device to test, because there is no comma device. What remains to verify is the panel board's input lag (section 4.5) and whether the ZED and OAK-D together saturate the carrier's USB controller at full rate (verify in Phase 1, by running both at once).

8.5 Display notes

The display research for this plan asked three questions: whether an automotive HUD-grade TFT module can be bought as a single unit, which industrial sunlight-readable panels exist above 2,000 cd/m², and what the small HDMI panel kits actually deliver. The sources are in Appendix D. The conclusion that drives the plan is that no purchasable panel reaches daylight without the backlight build, and that the HUD-grade module is an experiment, not a part.

ClassWhat was found on 2026-10-21
Automotive HUD-grade TFT modulesKyocera lists HUD panels of 1.12, 1.8, 2.6 and 3.1 inches (300 × 160 to 800 × 480; transmittance 6.2–8.5%; contrast to 1,700:1; −40 to +105 °C) without luminance, part numbers or distributor stock. Tianma shows a 4.1-inch 1280 × 640 HUD panel with a collimated backlight and a 12-inch HUD panel quoted at 12,000 nits. JDI, BOE, AUO, Innolux and Sharp publish no HUD panel datasheet. Teardowns: a Continental BMW HUD with a JDI 1.8-inch 480 × 240 panel (40.8 × 20.4 mm) and 15 white LEDs; a Nippon Seiki BMW HUD with a 3.15-inch 800 × 480 IPS panel and two 4 mm LEDs. A backlight-module vendor quotes 1,250,000 cd/m² at 6.6 W for a 3.1-inch HUD backlight. No online marketplace listing of a bare HUD TFT with a brightness figure was found.
Used OEM HUD unitsListed on eBay at about $80 (BMW F10), $90–275 (Mazda3, Hyundai Ioniq 5, Mercedes), up to $450–650 (Audi A6, Mazda CX-5). No write-up exists of anyone driving one of these panels from their own board; the interfaces are LVDS or parallel RGB and the panels are undocumented. A worthwhile experiment: buy the cheapest, identify the panel, and try an LVDS bridge board. Not on the critical path.
Industrial sunlight-readable panelsNothing at 4.3, 5 or 7 inches reaches 2,000 cd/m². The brightest found is a Litemax 7-inch at 1,600; the common class is 1,000–1,100 (Newhaven 5-inch HDMI at 1,100, $74.75; Crystalfontz 4.3-inch 800 × 480 at 1,000, $24.20; Riverdi 7-inch at 1,000, $44.45; Orient Display 5- and 7-inch at 1,000, $24–49). Orient Display offers 1,500–2,000 as a custom build with films and a changed LED set, which is the same thing this plan does itself.
Hobby HDMI kitsNo 5-inch 1080p kit. Wisecoco 5.5-inch 1920 × 1080 HDMI at 450 cd/m², $95. Waveshare 5-inch 720 × 1280 at 350 cd/m², $59.99, display area 62.1 × 110.4 mm; Waveshare 5-inch 1024 × 600 "high brightness" at 800 cd/m², $63.63. Small panels of this kind are edge-lit (a 4.3-inch datasheet shows 18 LED chips along one edge), which is what the backlight build removes.
Numbers used in the backlight designColour TFT transmittance 3–8%, with 7.4% quoted as typical in an LG patent; HUD-grade cells 6.2–8.5%. 3M prism film "effective transmission" about 1.5 per film, 2.8 for a stack of two with a reflective polariser; films rated to 95 °C. High-power white LEDs at 158–195 lm/W at 700 mA and 85 °C junction (Cree XP-G4, Lumileds HL2X). Edge-lit light guides are 40–60% efficient. Liquid-crystal clearing points near 75 °C for typical mixtures and above 100 °C for automotive ones.
