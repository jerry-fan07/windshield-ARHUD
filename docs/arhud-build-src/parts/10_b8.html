B8Stage 1 combiner in the car (gate G4)

SolvesA real in-car virtual image before the custom mirror exists (2.1), the first registration on the road (2.3, 2.10)
Weeks4–8
ClosesGate G4: static error at 30 m ≤ 1.0°; ribbon on the lane through curves at 25 m/s, at night
NeedsB7 (everything else in the car), B6 (renderer)
ProducesA working night and overcast HUD at about 4.2 m; the demo fallback if the mirror is late
PeopleBuilder, driver, safety driver

What this stage solves, intuitively. Optical Stage 1 makes the virtual image with a cheap flat Fresnel lens instead of the custom mirror, and shows it on a separate glass (the combiner) standing on the dash, instead of on the windshield. The panel sits just inside the lens's focal length, so the lens works as a magnifying glass and the image appears about 4 m ahead. It is dim (night and overcast only) and has ring artefacts, but it puts a registered overlay on a real road weeks before Stage 2 can, and it is the demonstration if the mirror is late.

B8.2 Build and install

  1. Build the enclosure. Blacken every inside face. The panel lies face-up at the bottom; the lens rests on the top ledge, 280 mm above the panel's face (measure to the lens's grooved structure, not to its frame).
  2. Orient the Fresnel: lens makers specify which face goes toward the longer conjugate (here the viewer's side). Follow the maker's note; if in doubt, try both ways on the bench and keep the one that gives the sharper grid.
  3. Fit the combiner's hinge arms to the enclosure's front. Edge the glass with the rubber U-channel and clamp it in the arms. Set its tilt to about 40–45° from horizontal, top toward the driver: light rising from the lens then reflects back toward the eye.
  4. Set the enclosure on the anti-slip mat on the dash in front of the driver, low enough that the combiner's top edge stays below the driver's line of sight to the road beyond 10 m except through the glass. Strap it. Pull-test it as in B13.2.No movement at 1.5 × weight
  5. Configure hudd for Stage 1: crop 80 × 40 mm on the monitor (about 872 × 436 px at 277 ppi), one reflection (set the flip with the test card), focal length from the 16° × 8° field.
  6. At night, from the eye point, tilt the combiner until the window is centred in the line of sight. Measure the image distance by parallax (Figure B12.1) with the pole at 4.2 m; move the panel up or down a few millimetres to bring the image to about 4 m. Expect about 15× magnification: the 80 × 40 mm crop appears 1.2 × 0.6 m wide.

A loose combiner is a projectile

Glass standing on the dash can strike the driver in a hard stop. Edge it, lock its hinge, strap the enclosure, pull-test it, and keep it out of the steering wheel's and the passenger airbag's paths. Stage 1 is also the more legally exposed configuration (plan, 11.2): closed course only.

B8.3 Calibrate and drive

  1. Set up the 30 m board (B13.3 A); camera at the eye point. Photograph the panel grid through the combiner against the ground grid; fit the warp mesh (a 2-D correction like B13.3 C, but on a single plane: Stage 1 has no custom mirror to trace).
  2. Refine the eye point with the board's rings.
  3. Measure static registration at 30 m with the camera, then with the camera moved ±75 mm on the rail, with the head tracker off and on.
  4. Drive the course at night and in overcast at up to 25 m/s with prediction on. The passenger watches the audience view's residuals; the driver reports where the ribbon leaves the lane.

B8.4 Gate G4

  • Static error at 30 m ≤ 1.0° from the eye point.
  • The ribbon stays on the lane through the course's curves at 25 m/s, at night.
  • Decision recorded: whether Stage 1 will be shown, and whether Stage 2 continues on schedule.
