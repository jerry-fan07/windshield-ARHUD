Before anything else

How to use this guide

The guide has three parts. Part I explains the idea: what the driver sees, the problems that stand between an ordinary display and that picture, and the component that solves each one. Read it once, end to end, before buying anything; every later instruction refers back to it. Part II sets up the workshop and lists every material, tool and instrument, with the build stage that first needs it. Part III is the build, in fourteen build stages (B1 to B14), each with the same layout: what the stage solves, what you will have at the end, materials, tools, a numbered procedure with checks, the acceptance test, and what to do when something goes wrong. The appendices hold wiring schedules, pin maps, file formats, print settings and blank test sheets.

Two words are used carefully. A build stage (B1 to B14) is a chapter of this guide. An optical stage (Stage 0, Stage 1, Stage 2) is one of the plan's three ways of making the picture: a bench beamsplitter, an in-car Fresnel magnifier and combiner, and the final relay mirror into the windshield. Gates G0 to G7 are the plan's pass/fail tests; a build stage is finished when its gate passes, and a failed gate is recorded, not waived.

Design basis: what this guide builds, and where it differs from the plan PDF

The guide builds the plan's design (HUD only, no driving functions, one Jetson, a TFT panel with a custom backlight, a relay mirror into the stock windshield) to the layout in the simulator (docs/arhud-sim/js/geom.js) and the printed housing in docs/arhud-cad, because those are the only geometry with print files. Where the simulations found a defect in the plan's numbers, the guide follows the simulation and says so. None of these findings has been measured on hardware yet; the real-car ray trace in B1 supersedes all of them.
