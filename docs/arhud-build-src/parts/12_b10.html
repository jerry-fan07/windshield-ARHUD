What this stage solves, intuitively. These are the only custom or specialised optical parts. The concave mirror is the magnifier and sets the image distance and shape; the fold, the IR-cut plate and the dust-cover polariser are the optical half of the sun defence. Each one is checked on arrival against what was ordered, because a wrong figure or a reversed coating is cheap to catch on the bench and expensive to discover in the car.

The light budget does not yet include these parts

The plan's path from panel to eye, {{path_ws}}, counts the two mirrors and the windshield only. Light also passes the dust-cover glass (about 92% uncoated), its polariser (perhaps 85–90% for light along its axis assumed), the hot-mirror plate (about 90% assumed) and, if fitted, the retarder (about 92%). Together that is roughly 0.65–0.75 of the plan's figure, and the {{l_panel_design}} cd/m² panel would deliver about 1,250–1,450 cd/m² at the eye, under the 1,500 target. Mitigations, in order: buy the anti-reflection-coated cover glass and the highest-transmission polariser; measure each part's transmission on arrival (B10.4) and recompute; and build the backlight to about 13,000 cd/m² rather than 10,000 (B9), which the crossed prism films make possible at about 8 W of LED if their assumed gain holds.

B10.2 Handling first-surface mirrors

  • The reflecting coating is on the front, exposed. It scratches under a fingernail and a fingerprint etches it within days. Wear gloves; hold the mirror by its edges or its back.
  • Store face down on a clean lint-free cloth, or in its shipping case. Never face-up toward a window.
  • Dust: blow it off. Fingerprints: a single, light drag of lens tissue wet with isopropyl alcohol, no pressure, never a dry wipe and never a circular rub.

B10.3 Inspect the concave mirror

  1. Unpack it gloved on the optics bench. Check the outline and thickness with calipers against the order; check the edges for chips.
  2. Look at the coating under raking light (a torch nearly parallel to the surface, in a dark room): note scratches, digs and coating voids. Photograph them; anything inside the traced footprint larger than about 0.5 mm goes back to the shop.
  3. Check the curvature. Make a point source: a white LED behind a 0.5 mm hole in black card. Stand the mirror upright on the bench; put the source near its axis at about its radius of curvature (about 0.64–0.69 m for this design) and look at the light returned onto a white card held just beside the source. Slide source and card together along the axis. A sphere returns a point at one distance, its radius. A toroid returns a horizontal line at one distance and a vertical line at another: these are its two radii. Measure each from the mirror's vertex.
  4. Compare with the order: within about 1% of each radius for a toroid. For a freeform, compare the shop's measurement report with the sag table instead; the functional test is B12's image.Radii or report match the order
  5. Check the reflectance: light a white card evenly, read its luminance with the meter directly, then via the mirror at a small angle from the same distance. The ratio is the reflectance (the plan assumes 0.90).Reflectance recorded

B10.4 Inspect the fold, the IR-cut plate and the cover parts

  1. Cold mirror: identify the coated face (pen-tip test, B12.3). Look at a white lamp in it at about 35°: the reflection should look neutral white; a strong colour cast means a coating designed for a different angle. Measure its reflectance for s light: reference polariser in front of the meter, axis across the plane of incidence.
  2. Hot-mirror plate: look at an incandescent lamp or the sun's reflection off a wall through it; it should look almost clear. Measure its visible transmission with the meter against a lit white card.
  3. Polariser: find and mark its pass axis. Look through it at the glare off a glossy horizontal surface (a table, a wet road) at a low angle: that glare is mostly horizontally polarised (s). It is brightest when the pass axis is horizontal. Mark the axis on the film's edge with an arrow.
  4. Measure the polariser's transmission for polarised light: the panel's own output (stock kit) through the film with the axes aligned, against the panel alone. Record it; it enters the budget above.

B10.5 Laminate the dust cover

  1. Clean the glass on both faces with isopropyl alcohol and lint-free wipes; work in the least dusty room available, with the air still.
  2. Lay the polariser over the glass's underside with its pass axis parallel to the glass edge that will lie across the car. Mark that edge "across".
  3. Peel back 30 mm of the liner (or of the OCA's liner), tack that edge, and squeegee slowly toward the far edge while peeling, so no air is trapped.
  4. Trim the film 1 mm inside the glass's edge with a fresh blade. Small bubbles at the dust cover are out of focus and only scatter a little light; large ones or creases mean starting again.
  5. Check: through the cover, a polarised-sunglasses lens rotated to vertical blocks the bench rig's image if the cover passes s (horizontal) light.Cover's pass axis across the car
