What this stage solves, intuitively. Everything except the optics goes into the car now, wired the way it will stay. The overlay is first drawn onto the road camera's own picture on a monitor in the passenger footwell: if the lines sit on the paint in that picture, perception, timing and compensation all work, and any error later in Stage 1 or Stage 2 belongs to the optics or the eye calibration. Nobody in the driver's seat watches the monitor.

B7.2 Find the right sockets

The converter can only shut the Jetson down cleanly after the key is turned off if its input stays live after the key is turned off. Many cars switch their front accessory socket with the ignition; some have a socket that is always live (often in the boot or the centre console), and some keep sockets live for a few minutes after key-off.

  1. With a multimeter, test every socket: key out, key in accessory position, engine running, and for ten minutes after key-off. Note each socket's fuse rating from the owner's manual or the fuse box lid.
  2. Use an always-live socket for feed 1 (the converter's VIN) and any switched socket for the ignition-sense lead. Use a third socket (or the switched one, if its fuse allows) for feed 2.
  3. If no socket stays live after key-off, the converter loses its input with the key: then shut the HUD down from the laptop (or a button that runs poweroff) before turning the key, and set the converter's off delay to 0 so it never waits on a missing input. Record which applies.

B7.3 Configure the converter

Set the DCDC-USB-200's jumpers on its configuration header (J7), with the converter unpowered, then power-cycle it. The manual's table and its USB tool agree on these settings; the product page differs on one output step, so confirm the output with the meter before connecting anything.

B7.5 Install the footwell plate

  1. Fix the base plate in the passenger footwell so it cannot move (B3.1): the GNSS board's dead reckoning needs it rigid. Leave the area under the passenger's feet free if someone will ride there.
  2. Mount on it: the Jetson (vents unobstructed, fan side open), the DC-DC converter in the Jetson fan's airflow, the GNSS board (already square to the car from B3.4), the switch, and the 5 V buck.
  3. Wire feed 1: accessory plug, 10 A fuse within 150 mm of the plug, 1.5 mm² to the converter's VIN; ignition lead with its 1 A fuse to the converter's ignition pin (follow the board's silkscreen labels, not wire colours: the manual's colours disagree). Common ground.
  4. Converter output (16 V) to: the Jetson's DC plug (centre positive: check with the meter before plugging in), the LED drivers' 2 A fused feed (run to the dash beside the HUD housing), and the 5 V buck.
  5. 5 V buck outputs to: the panel board's USB power input (the Wisecoco kit is powered by 5 V over USB; its connector differs by board revision), the controller's 5 V terminal, the OAK-D's Y-adapter power leg, and the housing fan.
  6. Data: ZED 2i and GNSS board to the Jetson's USB 3 ports; OAK-D's data leg to a third; controller's USB to the fourth; HDMI from the Jetson to the panel board (or to monitor 1 for gate G3); Ethernet from the Jetson to the switch, switch to mini PC 2.
  7. Tie every cable to the plate or along the trim with clips; nothing loose near the pedals or the seat rails; no cable across the passenger's airbag door.Cables secured; nothing near the pedals

B7.6 The audience view

  1. Clamp the headrest bracket to the passenger headrest's posts and mount monitor 2 facing the rear seats; mount mini PC 2 behind it. Power both from the PD charger on feed 2.
  2. Give mini PC 2 the static address 192.168.50.3/24. On the Jetson, audienced serves the MJPEG stream (the rendered HUD frame, the driver camera with the eye point, and the live numbers); mini PC 2 opens it full screen in a browser in kiosk mode at boot.

B7.7 Calibrate the camera and the eye point

  1. Road camera in the car frame: the 30 m target board procedure (B13.3 A). It is the same procedure as Stage 2's, done now so G3 measures perception with the final camera geometry.
  2. Driver camera pose and the nominal eye point (B13.3 B and B1.3).
  3. Write calibration.yaml and commit it.

B7.8 Drive with the overlay on the monitor

  1. Sign the installation checklist (Appendix F).
  2. Set monitor 1 on its stand in the passenger footwell, facing the passenger. Run hudd in camera-overlay mode on it: the road camera's live image with the ribbon, lines and chevron drawn on it by the full pipeline, with prediction on.
  3. Measure the converter's input current with a clamp meter at idle, driving, and with the backlight at full (when it exists) and update B7.4.
  4. Drive the course for 20 minutes at up to 25 m/s. The passenger watches the overlay against the paint and the lead car and logs any excursion; hudStats logs the residuals and ages. A phone records its own GNSS speed for comparison.

B7.9 Gate G3

  • Over 20 minutes at up to 25 m/s with prediction on, the overlay stays within 0.5° of the lane paint and the lead vehicle in the camera image.
  • GNSS speed agrees with the phone's within 1 m/s while moving.
  • Key-off shutdown works in the car, and the checklist is signed.

SymptomResponse
