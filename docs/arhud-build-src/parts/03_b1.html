Why this comes first. The windshield is an optical element of the HUD: its angle sets where the mirror must sit and where the sun can enter, its curvature changes the image distance and shape, and its thickness sets the ghost. The mirror's shape therefore cannot be specified until the car is chosen and measured, and the mirror has the longest lead time in the project. Every week spent here before ordering the mirror is a week on the critical path, so do the measurements in one session and run the trace immediately.

B1.3 Fix the driver's eye point

  1. Park the car on level ground. Put the angle gauge on the floor beside the car (or on the door sill) and record the reading as the ground reference; every later angle is measured relative to it.
  2. Seat the person who will drive the demos in their normal driving position: seat, backrest, head restraint and steering wheel as they will be on the day. Record all four settings (seat-track notch, backrest angle, wheel reach and height) in the log; the calibration is only valid for them.
  3. Have the driver look straight ahead at a distant point. Hang the plumb bob from a point midway between their eyes (a second person holds it at the bridge of the nose, about 10 mm in front) and mark where it touches the floor mat with tape. That is the eye point's plan position.
  4. Measure the height of the eye midpoint above the ground with the tape (or the rangefinder from the floor plus the floor's height). The plan assumes 1.22 m. Record it.
  5. Measure the eye point's position relative to three fixed datums that do not move with the seat: the centre of the steering-wheel hub, the base of the mirror stalk, and the driver's B-pillar seat-belt anchor. These let you find the eye point again without the driver.
  6. Ask the driver to sway left, right, up and down as they would in normal driving; note the range. The design eye box is 130 mm wide and 70 mm tall around the nominal point.

B1.4 Find and measure the HUD patch on the windshield

  1. Find the patch. Put the laser pointer on the tripod head at the nominal eye point, aimed straight ahead (parallel to the car's centreline) and 5° below horizontal, set with the angle gauge against the ground reference. Where the spot meets the glass is the centre of the HUD patch. Mark it on the outside of the glass with a small piece of painter's tape (never write on the inner surface). Measure the distance from the eye point to the spot along the beam with the rangefinder or tape; the plan assumes 750 mm.
  2. Rake. Hold the angle gauge flat on the inner glass at the patch, its long edge in the car's vertical centre plane through the patch (pointing up the glass). Read the angle; subtract the ground reference. Repeat three times, lifting and re-seating the gauge each time, and average. Repeat 100 mm left and right of the patch to see how fast the rake changes.Rake recorded at three points; spread under 0.5°
  3. Laminate thickness. Look for the glass label in a lower corner (maker, model number, DOT code); a maker's catalogue may list the thickness. Then measure it: in a darkened car, shine the laser at the patch from a known angle θi (measure with the angle gauge, 50–70° from the glass normal) and hold the white card where the reflection lands, about 300 mm away. You will see two spots, one from each outer surface (a faint third from the interlayer may appear). Measure their separation d perpendicular to the beam. The thickness is t = d / (2 tan θt cos θi), with θt from Snell's law, sin θt = sin θi / 1.52. At θi = 65° and the assumed 4.96 mm, d = 3.1 mm. Average three angles.Thickness recorded, with the label's part number
  4. Curvature. Lay the straightedge on the inner glass across the patch, horizontal (across the car). The glass curves away from it toward the middle or the ends. Slide feeler gauges under the straightedge at its centre to find the gap s (the sagitta) while both ends touch. The radius is R = L² / (8 s): with L = 300 mm, a 4 m radius gives s = 2.8 mm and an 8 m radius 1.4 mm. Repeat with the straightedge running up the glass along the rake. These are Rh (lateral) and Rv (vertical). Repeat both 100 mm above and below the patch.Rh and Rv recorded at three heights
  5. Cowl and roof. Measure the height of the windshield's bottom edge (the cowl) and top edge above the ground, and their fore-aft distance from the eye point. The simulator uses a cowl at 0.80 m and the roof edge at 1.385 m.

Why the curvature matters so much

The Cycles cross-check traced the plan's toroid mirror against a windshield curved with 4 m lateral and 8 m vertical radii: the virtual image turned into a real image about 12 m ahead and the window grew to about 10° × 7.5°. A freeform mirror refitted for that glass recovered a flat image at 7.4 to 7.6 m. A real windshield is curved; a mirror designed for flat glass will not work in it. The two radii you measure here are inputs to the mirror's design, not details.

B1.5 Map the dash

  1. Mark the housing's footprint with painter's tape: centred on the driver's centreline (the simulator puts the car's centreline 370 mm to the driver's right), its front edge where a vertical from the HUD patch's lower region meets the dash, about 460 mm long and 370 mm wide.
  2. Take contour-gauge profiles of the dash top along three fore-aft lines inside the footprint (left edge, centre, right edge), and one lateral profile at the front and one at the rear. Trace each onto paper with the datum marked, or photograph it on a grid.
  3. Measure the instrument cluster hood: its top height, its front edge's position, and its width, relative to the eye point.
  4. Measure the vertical gap from the dash to the glass at the front of the footprint and at 100 mm intervals backward. The CAD keeps the housing 8 mm under the glass.
  5. Photograph the dash from above and from the driver's seat with a rule in view; photograph the defroster vents, sensors and sockets.
  6. Optional: scan the dash, cluster hood and the inner glass. import_scan.py (in docs/arhud-blender) joins, cleans and fits a scan to the simulator's frame from paired landmark points; it reached about 1.4 mm on the simulated test scan.

B1.6 Run the ray trace

The trace takes the measurements and returns the mirror's shape, the positions of the panel, fold and mirror, the image distance across the window, the window's size, the eye box, the ghost, and the sun-entry patch. The project already has the tools; this procedure feeds them the real car. Allow one to three days; record every input and output in the log.

  1. Enter the measurements. In docs/arhud-plan-tft-src/calc.py, replace the assumed values with the measured ones: RAKE, LOOKDOWN (keep 5° unless the patch had to move), L_EYE_GLASS, T_GLASS, and H_CAM (the eye height). Run python3 sim_params.py to regenerate the simulator's parameters and python3 spec.py for the specification.
  2. Enter the glass curvature in docs/arhud-sim/js/geom.js: GLASS = { Rh: …, Rv: … } in metres. Check that GLASS_TO_MIRROR (0.31 m) still clears the glass: the simulator writes the mirror's clearance into its notes (Vehicle mode).
  3. Fit the mirror. In docs/arhud-blender: node dump_layout.mjs, then python3 freeform_mirror.py glassRh=<Rh> glassRv=<Rv> name=freeform_car. It fits a nine-term polynomial mirror with the panel's tilt and shift free, against a 7.5 m target. Read the fitted image-distance range across the window and the window size from its output.
  4. Cross-check in Cycles (Blender, headless): Blender -b -P optics_check.py with the same glass radii and mirror. It measures the image distance dot by dot by stereo parallax, the ghost, the eye box, and the field independently of the fit. Accept the design only if the two agree.
  5. Sun. python3 sun_focus.py reports the solar spot on the panel (size, irradiance) and the patch of sky whose sun reaches the crop and the whole cell. Record the cell patch: it is the sun-watch rule's input.
  6. Export for the quote. Blender -b -P mirror_export.py -- name=freeform_car writes the sag table (CSV) and an STL of the mirror surface into export/.
  7. Regenerate the housing. In docs/arhud-cad: node dump_layout.mjs, then ~/.venvs/arhud-cad/bin/python make.py. The beam check must report 0 blocked rays; the envelope check must clear the measured glass, hood and dash. Edit hudcad/geo.py for the real dash profile and hood.

B1.7 Request mirror quotes

Send the request the day the trace is accepted. A quote does not commit the order; ask for lead time explicitly, because it sets the daylight demo date. Shops that list custom concave optics to 500 mm include Avantier and Shanghai Optics (plan, section 8.2). Include:

  • Clear aperture and outline (the plan's 300 × 200 mm, or what the trace gives), substrate thickness (the CAD's strongback assumes about 12 mm), edge finish.
  • The surface: the sag table and STL from mirror_export.py, with its coordinate frame. If the shop can make only toroids, give Rt and Rs from the trace (for the flat-glass layout: {{lay_rt}} and {{lay_rs}} mm). State the speed: about f/1.1, much faster than catalogue parts; ask whether they will figure it.
  • Figure tolerance in the form the image needs. A local slope error α deflects the reflected ray by 2α, which the eye sees directly as an angular error. Keeping that under 0.05° (a sixth of the head-motion budget) asks for local slope errors under about 1.5 arcminutes; ask what the shop can hold and how they measure it.
  • Coating: first-surface protected aluminium (or enhanced aluminium), reflectance ≥ 90% across 450–650 nm (the plan's assumed 0.90), and its reflectance in the near infrared if offered.
  • Ask the shop to report the solar spot at the panel from their own model of the surface (the plan's request): a figure change can spread the spot several-fold at no cost to the image.
  • Quantity 1, plus a price for a second as a spare; lead time; packing for a first-surface mirror.

B1.8 Place the first orders, book the venue, write the checklist

  1. Order everything in Chapter 6 marked week 0: it does not depend on the car.
  2. Agree the venue and dates for recordings (week 1–2), live drives (week 3 onward) and rehearsals. Confirm permission for a lead car and a volunteer pedestrian if the hazard demo needs them.
  3. Confirm who signs off the installation (the plan names the university's risk office for anything beyond a closed course) and give them Appendix F as the draft checklist. Agree the safety driver.

B1.9 Gate G0

  • The car is named and its measurements are in the log: eye point, rake, thickness, two radii, cowl and roof, dash profiles, hood.
  • The ray trace and the Cycles check agree, and their outputs are recorded.
  • Mirror quotes are requested with the surface, tolerance and lead-time questions above.
  • Week-0 orders are placed.
  • The venue is agreed, and the installation checklist exists and has been seen by whoever signs it off.

B1.10 If it goes wrong

SymptomResponse
Rake outside 25–35°The design can still work, but the mirror moves and the sun patch changes. Run the trace and check the mirror clears the glass and the housing fits the dash before committing.
Double-spot measurement shows three or more spots, or spots not parallelThe glass may have a wedge interlayer (a factory HUD windshield). Measure the angle between the two reflected beams over a longer throw (2 m) and give the wedge angle to the trace.
The housing does not fit under the glassShorten the path: a shorter-focal-length mirror with a smaller crop (plan, section 6.6 table: a 40 mm crop needs f ≈ 217 mm), at the cost of fewer pixels per degree. Re-run the trace.
Fit and Cycles disagree by more than about 0.5 m in image distanceDo not order. Check the layout file is current (re-run dump_layout.mjs), that both used the same glass radii, and that the mirror mode is the same in both.
No shop will figure an f/1.1 freeformAsk for a toroid; accept the curved image surface and rely on the lookup-table projection (B13), or fall back to the Stage 1 demo (B8) while a better mirror is sourced.
