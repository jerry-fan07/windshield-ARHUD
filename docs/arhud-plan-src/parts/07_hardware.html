The two largest uncertainties are the mirror, which is a quote and not a price, and the luminance meter, which is needed for the contrast criterion and cannot be replaced by a lux meter. The mirror cost could move the total by several thousand dollars; the meter is a fixed cost that the validation depends on.

8.3 Power and wiring

The Jetson runs from the car's 12 V bus through an ignition-sensed DC-DC converter. Ignition sensing lets the Jetson see the key-off signal and shut down cleanly. Without that, the Jetson loses power mid-write and can corrupt its filesystem, which is the reason the plan lists the Mini-Box as a required part and not an optional one. The monitors take power from two USB-C PD car chargers. The comma four stays on its own car harness.

One wiring question is not answered in the plan. The Jetson drives monitor 1 over HDMI, and the JetPack 5.1.3 record shows a single HDMI output in use. Monitor 2, for the audience view, needs a second output, either a second HDMI or DisplayPort on the carrier, or a USB or network path into a second display device. unverified The carrier's display outputs must be checked before the audience view is designed.

The Ethernet path has one more check. The plan says the comma four's USB 3.1 port powers the USB-C Ethernet adapter, and whether that port runs in host mode with a suitable USB Ethernet driver has not been tested (verify). If it does not, the fallback is the comma four's Wi-Fi hotspot, with the jitter cost noted in section 4.5.
