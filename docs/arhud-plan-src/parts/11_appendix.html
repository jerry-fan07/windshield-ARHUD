ADerivations

Each formula used in the plan, with the worked numbers. The values are the ones printed in the text and computed in the build script, so a reader can check any figure by hand.

A.1 Fresnel reflectance

For light from air (n₁ = 1) reaching glass (n₂ = 1.52) at incidence angle θᵢ, with the transmitted angle θt from Snell's law, the reflected fractions are:

sin θt = sin θᵢ ÷ n₂ Rs = [(cos θᵢ − n₂ cos θt) ÷ (cos θᵢ + n₂ cos θt)]² Rp = [(n₂ cos θᵢ − cos θt) ÷ (n₂ cos θᵢ + cos θt)]²

At θᵢ = 65°: sin θt = 0.906 ÷ 1.52 = 0.596, so θt = 36.6°. Then Rs = {{rs65}} and Rp = {{rp65}}. Rp is zero at the Brewster angle, arctan(1.52) = {{brewster}}°. These are per surface; a laminated windshield has two.

A.2 Mirror equation, Stage 2

For a concave mirror of focal length f, an object at distance u in front of the mirror forms an image at distance v, with 1/u + 1/v = 1/f. Magnification is M = −v/u. When the object is inside the focal length, v is negative and the image is virtual, on the object's side, and magnified.

Virtual image at 7.5 m, f = 330 mm: 1/u = 1/f + 1/7500 = 0.0030303 + 0.0001333 so u = 316.1 mm M = 7500 ÷ 316.1 = 23.7 image width = 60 mm × 23.7 = 1.42 m so field 2·atan(0.711 ÷ 7.5) = 10.8° image height = 30 mm × 23.7 = 0.71 m so field 2·atan(0.356 ÷ 7.5) = 5.4° Revision 2's u = 346 mm, f = 330 mm: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 0.0030303 − 0.0028902 = 0.0001401 so v = +7.14 m (real) |M| = 7140 ÷ 346 = 20.6

The second case is the one revision 2 had. Its positive v means a real image, 7.1 m from the mirror, which cannot be seen as a virtual image at 7.5 m. The single-mirror model also omits the windshield, which is covered in section 6.6.

A.3 Lens equation, Stage 1

A thin converging lens of focal length f, with an object at distance u on the object side, follows the same equation. With f = 300 mm and u = 280 mm:

1/v = 1/300 − 1/280 = −0.000238 so v = −4200 mm (virtual, object side) M = 4200 ÷ 280 = 15 image = 80 mm × 15 = 1.2 m wide, 40 mm × 15 = 0.6 m high field = 2·atan(0.6 ÷ 4.2) = 16.3° wide, 2·atan(0.3 ÷ 4.2) = 8.2° high pixels per degree: (80 mm ÷ 0.0917 mm per px) ÷ 16.3° = 872 ÷ 16.3 ≈ 54

A Fresnel lens has the same equation as the thin lens it approximates. The grooves change the aberrations, which is why the plan expects ring artefacts.

A.4 Window size

The width of a window of field θ at distance D is 2D·tan(θ/2). At 7.5 m, the 10° window is 2 × 7.5 × tan 5° = 1.31 m wide. The 4° height is 2 × 7.5 × tan 2° = 0.52 m, and the 5° height is 0.65 m. The corrected 10.8° × 5.4° window is {{img2_w}} × {{img2_h}}.

A.5 Luminance from flux, and the budget chain

For a Lambertian emitter of area A and luminance L, the total flux into the hemisphere is πLA. Inverting it, a transmissive diffuser that passes a fraction η of flux Φ has luminance L = Φη ÷ (πA). For Φ = 2000 lm, η = 0.5, and A = 0.060 × 0.030 = 0.0018 m²:

L = 2000 × 0.5 ÷ (π × 0.0018) = {{L_DLP}} cd/m²

Each optical element then multiplies the luminance by its reflectance or transmittance. An ideal imaging mirror changes the image size but not its luminance, so the chain is a product of fractions:

Combiner path: L_eye = L_source × 0.30 = {{dlp_comb}} Windshield path: L_eye = L_source × 0.24 = {{dlp_ws}} Windshield + 2 mirrors L_eye = L_source × 0.90 × 0.90 × 0.24 = {{dlp_ws_mirr}} With polariser sheet L_eye = L_source × 0.90 × 0.90 × 0.24 × 0.42 = {{dlp_ws_mirr_pol}}

The 0.90 mirror figure and the 0.42 polariser transmission are assumptions. The polariser is needed because a rear-projection diffuser depolarises the light that passes through it (section 6.3). The windshield factor is the s-polarised Rs from A.1 for one surface, rounded to 0.24. A laminated windshield reflects from both of its air-glass surfaces: the first reflects Rs, and the second returns (1 − Rs)² Rs after two passes through the first. With the wedge overlapping the two images at the design eye point, the combined reflection is about {{rs65_two}}, so 0.24 is the conservative figure.

A.6 Contrast ratio and the noon target

The plan defines contrast as (overlay + background) ÷ background. For a background of 10,000 cd/m² and a required ratio of 1.15, the overlay must supply 0.15 × 10,000 = 1,500 cd/m² at the eye.

A.7 Dimming range

day target ÷ night ceiling = 1,500 ÷ 50 = {{dim_day_night}} full output ÷ night ceiling = {{dlp_ws}} ÷ 50 = {{dim_full_night}} daylight fraction, bare windshield path = 1,500 ÷ {{dlp_ws}} = {{dlp_day_frac_raw}} daylight fraction, mirrors and polariser = 1,500 ÷ {{dlp_ws_mirr_pol}} = {{dlp_day_frac}}

A.8 Parallax from head shift

A head shift of Δ moves the line of sight to a point at distance d by an angle atan(Δ ÷ d), if the image is not corrected. For Δ = 0.150 m: at 7.5 m, atan(0.02) = 1.15°; at 10 m, atan(0.015) = 0.86°; at 30 m, atan(0.005) = 0.29°.

A.9 Motion compensation

A camera at height h = 1.22 m sights a road point at horizontal distance d. The depression angle is θ = atan(h ÷ d). At 30 m/s, the car travels 3.0 m in 100 ms, so the point's distance drops from 10 m to 7.0 m:

Uncompensated: θ(10 m) = atan(1.22 ÷ 10) = {{dep0}}° θ(7.0 m) = atan(1.22 ÷ 7.0) = {{dep1}}° change = {{uncomp}}° Residual after prediction, for unmodelled acceleration a = 1 m/s² over t = 0.1 s: Δx = ½ a t² = 5.0 mm Δθ ≈ h ÷ (d² + h²) × Δx = 1.22 ÷ 101.5 × 0.005 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ rad = {{resid_deg}}°

The residual is small because prediction removes the large term, the car's own travel. What remains is what the model did not foresee.

A.10 Latency sums

The four timed stages sum to a minimum of 50 + 1 + 8 + 8 = {{lat_min}} ms and a maximum of 80 + 3 + 16 + 40 = {{lat_max}} ms. At 30 m/s these are 2.0 m and 4.2 m of travel, which the plan rounds to 2 to 4 m. The head tracker is excluded because it affects parallax, not the road-frame path.

A.11 Where a road point lands on the virtual plane

For a flat road and an eye at height h, the line of sight to a point at distance d crosses a plane at distance D at height h(1 − D ÷ d) above the road. With h = 1.22 m and D = 7.5 m: 1.22 × (1 − 7.5 ÷ 10) = 0.31 m for d = 10 m, and 1.22 × (1 − 7.5 ÷ 30) = 0.92 m for d = 30 m.

A.12 Sun focus estimate

The sun subtends 0.53° (9.25 mrad). Its focused image at the focal plane is about f × 9.25 mrad = 330 × 0.00925 = 3.1 mm across. For the ray-trace estimate, parallel on-axis rays strike a spherical mirror of radius 660 mm over a 300 × 200 mm aperture. Each reflected ray is intersected with the PGU plane 316 mm from the vertex. The radius that contains 90% of the rays is {{r90_pgu}} mm at that plane. The ideal, lossless concentration is the aperture area over the area of that spot:

C = (300 × 200 mm²) ÷ (π × r90²) ≈ {{conc_pgu}} (r90 = {{r90_pgu}} mm, unrounded in the calculation)

The trace gives a larger spot, {{r90_focus}} mm, at the paraxial focal plane, because marginal rays focus nearer the mirror (spherical aberration). The PGU plane is therefore close to the smallest blur, which is why it is the worst location for the diffuser. The estimate ignores the windshield and the sun's disk, so it is an upper bound.

collected power = 0.300 m × 0.200 m × 1,000 W/m² × {{ws_t_sun}} = {{sun_w}} spot diameter ≈ 2 × r90 = {{sun_spot_mm}} mm at the PGU plane

The 1,000 W/m² is direct sunlight on a clear day, and the {{ws_t_sun}} is an assumed windshield transmission for sunlight that covers both surfaces and the interlayer. The aperture is taken as fully lit, which is the worst case; the dash lip and the housing will shade part of it at most sun positions.
